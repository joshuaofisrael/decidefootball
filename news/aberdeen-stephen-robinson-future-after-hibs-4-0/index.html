Stephen Robinson's Aberdeen Future in Doubt After 4-0 Hibernian Defeat

By Decide Football · 2026-11-08

Stephen Robinson's position as Aberdeen head coach is under intense scrutiny after a 4-0 defeat to Hibernian at Pittodrie on Saturday, 7 November 2026, left the Dons in the Scottish Premiership relegation play-off place.

Aberdeen supporters called for Robinson's dismissal during the second half, but the manager insisted after the match that he remained in charge and would continue fighting for results unless the club decided otherwise.

Robinson Responds to Calls for His Dismissal

Speaking to BBC Scotland following the defeat, Robinson acknowledged that the performance and result were unacceptable.

"I don't know [what the implications may be], I don't make that decision," he said.

"I'm Aberdeen manager until told otherwise, I'll keep fighting until told otherwise."

Robinson also expressed confidence in his managerial ability while acknowledging that decisions about his future rested with the club's leadership.

He said he had not received any indication immediately after the match that his position had changed and expected to speak with senior figures, as he regularly does.

According to the Press and Journal, Aberdeen supporters chanted "You're getting sacked in the morning" as Hibernian extended their lead.

The newspaper reported that Robinson and his players were booed at full-time, following another difficult home performance.

As of Sunday morning, no confirmed announcement of Robinson's dismissal or a formal board review had emerged from the sources checked. Any suggestion that a decision has already been taken would therefore be premature.

Four Goals Deepen Aberdeen's Problems

Hibernian arrived at Pittodrie bottom of the Scottish Premiership but left with an emphatic victory that lifted them above Aberdeen on goal difference.

Former Aberdeen midfielder Jamie McGrath opened the scoring with a long-range strike in the 57th minute.

Callum Wright doubled the visitors' advantage in the 79th minute after goalkeeper Marius Müller saved an effort from Thibault Klidjé.

Nicky Cadden added a third in the 87th minute before Klidjé completed the scoring in stoppage time.

Sky Sports reported that the defeat was Aberdeen's fifth in their previous six league matches.

The Dons had opportunities during the first half, including a Jack Milne header against the crossbar, but failed to capitalise before Hibernian took control.

Aberdeen's own match report acknowledged that the hosts wasted several first-half chances and were comfortably beaten by the visitors' more clinical second-half performance.

The result left Aberdeen 11th in the table after 12 league matches, level on 11 points with Hibernian but behind on goal difference.

What Happens Next for Robinson?

Robinson was appointed Aberdeen manager in March 2026 after establishing a strong reputation at St Mirren, where he achieved three successive top-six finishes and won the Scottish League Cup.

However, BBC Sport Scotland reported that he has won only six of his first 21 Scottish Premiership matches in charge of Aberdeen, losing 11.

The BBC also highlighted concerns about recruitment and tactical consistency following a summer in which Aberdeen brought in 15 players.

Former Aberdeen captain Willie Miller criticised the team's performance and recruitment during BBC Sportsound coverage, while former Scotland midfielder Michael Stewart suggested on Premier Sports that the scale of the defeat could prove decisive for Robinson's future.

Those assessments are opinions from pundits rather than indications of an official club decision.

Aberdeen chief executive Adrian Bevington, appointed in September, now faces questions about whether Robinson should remain in charge.

The international break offers the club an opportunity to assess its position without an immediate league fixture, although no formal timetable for a decision has been confirmed.

For Robinson, the immediate reality is clear: Aberdeen are in the relegation play-off position, supporters have openly demanded change, and the club's leadership must determine whether the current manager can reverse the decline.
