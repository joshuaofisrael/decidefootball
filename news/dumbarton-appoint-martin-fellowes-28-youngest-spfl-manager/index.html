From kit boy to the youngest manager in SPFL history: why Dumbarton handed the job to a 28-year-old

By Decide Football · 2026-11-19

Dumbarton have made Martin Fellowes the youngest manager in SPFL history. The 28-year-old "doesn't believe his age will be an obstacle", Sky Sports News posted on X on Thursday morning. Sky's post spells his surname "Fellows"; BBC Sport and The Scotsman both use Fellowes.

The decision

Fellowes leaves his post as assistant manager and head of football operations at Stranraer, the Scottish League Two leaders, to take over a Dumbarton side six places below them, BBC Sport reports. He replaces Frank McKeown, who left last month. Director of football Danny Lennon had been in charge on an interim basis.

Fellowes spent almost ten years at East Kilbride, starting as a teenage kit boy and rising to a two-month spell as interim manager in 2023, when the club won the Lowland League Cup. He has coached with Scotland's under-19s, holds a Uefa A licence and still has a day job in office furniture sales, according to the BBC.

Why Dumbarton went for him

Lennon told The Scotsman that age was not a factor. "What really impressed us about Martin was his football knowledge, the clarity of his coaching philosophy, his understanding of player development and the passion and ambition he has for the game," he said. "I believe football should always be about recognising ability and potential, regardless of age or background."

What Fellowes says

"I know there will be plenty of criticism and there will be plenty of people sitting here today doubting that I'm capable of doing this job at 28," Fellowes told BBC Scotland. "I can only do that through winning games of football."

He does not plan to rip things up. "We're not going to reinvent the wheel," he said. "But if we can add the 5% to every different department and win games of football, I think over time people will understand that sometimes the age isn't a barrier but it can actually be an advantage."

The record he breaks

According to The Scotsman, Fellowes takes the mantle from Jim Duffy, who was 29 when he took charge of Falkirk in 1988. Rhys McCabe was the same age when he became player/manager at Airdrieonians. Ian Cathro, now at Saint-Etienne, was 30 when he took over at Hearts, the BBC notes.

What comes next

The BBC says Dumbarton are two points off the promotion places. Fellowes's first game is away to Spartans on Saturday.
