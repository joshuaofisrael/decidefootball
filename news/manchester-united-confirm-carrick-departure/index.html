Manchester United confirm Michael Carrick has left as head coach

By Decide Football · 2026-11-12

Manchester United have confirmed that Michael Carrick has left his role as men's first-team head coach, with the club in the Premier League relegation zone after one win from their opening ten league games. The club announced the decision on X on Thursday morning.

In its full statement, published by NBC Sports, the club said that after "a disappointing start to the season" it had concluded a change was needed, and that it intends to appoint a permanent successor as quickly as possible. The statement called Carrick a club legend and credited him with guiding the side to third place last season by winning 12 of the final 17 games.

How the decision was made

The Guardian reports that Carrick's exit followed a review led by director of football Jason Wilcox and a recommendation to Sir Jim Ratcliffe, and that the sacking was signed off at a board meeting involving the Glazer family. According to the same report, Carrick was given three games after a 3-0 home defeat by Bournemouth to show he was still the right man, and the club felt the performances against Roma, Aston Villa and Chelsea showed it was on the wrong path.

Carrick was given a contract to 2028 in May after his interim spell, The Guardian adds. He is the second head coach United have dismissed this calendar year, after Ruben Amorim left in January.

Who comes next

United have not named a replacement. The Athletic reports that United States head coach Mauricio Pochettino has emerged as the chosen candidate and that United had made formal contact with US Soccer, and The Guardian says he has a framework agreement with the club. Neither report has been confirmed by United. We covered the build-up to this decision in Wednesday's piece on the board's review.
