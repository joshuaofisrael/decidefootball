Thomas Frank set to take over at bottom-of-the-table Crystal Palace, according to Romano and The Athletic

By Decide Football · 2026-11-16

Update, 17 Nov: Crystal Palace confirmed Frank as manager later on Monday, on a two-and-a-half-year deal, The Guardian reports. The Guardian says chair Steve Parish led the talks and promised funds for January signings.

Crystal Palace are close to naming Thomas Frank as their new head coach. Transfer reporter Fabrizio Romano posted on X on Monday morning that a verbal agreement has been reached "with immediate effect", on a deal until June 2029, with formal steps to follow. The Athletic's David Ornstein wrote that Palace are finalising the deal, with details still to sort and talks continuing over the backroom team. Palace had not announced the appointment when we published.

Why Palace turned to Frank

Palace sacked Pierre Sage on Thursday after a 3-1 home defeat by Liverpool. The Athletic reports that the club are bottom of the Premier League with seven points from ten games and have conceded 24 league goals, the most in the division. It says Frank's work at Brentford, where he won promotion to the Premier League in 2021 and kept the club in the top flight for four seasons, is what appealed to Palace.

Frank, 53, has been out of work since Tottenham Hotspur sacked him in February after eight months in charge, with the club 16th, The Athletic adds.

The staff question

BBC Sport reporter Alex Howell writes that England coach Justin Cochrane, who worked with Frank at Brentford and Tottenham, is set to join his staff while keeping his role with Thomas Tuchel's England set-up. The BBC says there is still work to do to finalise the appointment.

What comes next

Palace return after the international break away at Coventry City on 21 November, according to The Athletic, so Frank would have only a few days with a full squad. The Athletic's analysis says he used both a 3-5-2 and a 4-3-3 at Brentford, and that the back three would be close to the system Palace have played under Oliver Glasner and Sage.

Our view: choosing a coach who knows the league well makes sense for a side in a relegation fight. But Frank's spell at Tottenham went badly, and Palace need results straight away.
