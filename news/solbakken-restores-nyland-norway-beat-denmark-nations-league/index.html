Solbakken brings Nyland back in goal and Norway come from 2-0 down to beat Denmark in the Nations League

By Decide Football · 2026-11-18

Norway head coach Ståle Solbakken switched goalkeepers again for Tuesday's Nations League League A match against Denmark in Copenhagen. Ørjan Håskjold Nyland returned to the starting line-up and Egil Selvik, who started Saturday's 3-1 win over Wales, was left out, NRK reported when the teams were confirmed. NRK's pundit Kristoffer Løkberg called it a bolt "from a clear sky" (our translation).

The decision

Solbakken made four changes from the Wales match. As well as Nyland, Alexander Sørloth, Patrick Berg and Leo Skiri Østigård came in, while Oscar Bobb, Thelonious Aasgaard and David Møller Wolfe dropped to the bench, NRK says. It was Sørloth's first appearance for Norway since the World Cup quarter-final against England.

Before the game Solbakken told TV 2 there was no fixed first and second choice in goal. "Egil has taken steps, but I have full confidence in Ørjan Nyland," he said, as quoted by NRK (our translation). He had warned at Monday's press conference that "it will not be the same team", according to NTB (our translation).

Norway lined up, according to NRK: Nyland; Julian Ryerson, Kristoffer Vassbakk Ajer, Østigård, Torbjørn Heggem; Berg, Sander Berge, Martin Ødegaard; Sørloth, Erling Haaland, Antonio Nusa.

How it worked out

Denmark went 2-0 up through Rasmus Højlund after 11 minutes and Pierre-Emile Højbjerg three minutes later. Haaland pulled one back on 37 minutes after Ødegaard won the ball from Denmark's goalkeeper, Ødegaard equalised six minutes from time, and Haaland scored an injury-time penalty to win it 3-2, the Norwegian FA reports. The result sends Norway into the Nations League quarter-finals, where they will face England, France or Germany, while Denmark go into the play-offs to stay in League A.

Our view: recalling a 36-year-old who has been second choice at RB Leipzig was a big call in a match with a quarter-final place at stake. Nyland conceded twice early, but Norway's comeback means the selection debate will carry on into next year rather than end in blame.
