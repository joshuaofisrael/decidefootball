Arteta tells Premier League leaders Arsenal to be 'much more chaotic' against low blocks

By Decide Football · 2026-11-09

Mikel Arteta wants Arsenal to stop chasing the perfect goal. After the 1-0 Premier League win over Hull, the Arsenal manager said his side must be "much more chaotic" against deep defences, the Guardian reported.

"Sometimes we try to score the perfect goal against low blocks, but we need to be much more chaotic than that. If we open the scoring much earlier also, it changes the game."
Mikel Arteta, quoted by the Guardian, 9 November 2026

The decision in focus

This is a tactical instruction from Arteta rather than a change of personnel. He is asking his players to accept messier, more direct routes to goal when opponents sit deep, instead of working the ball until the ideal chance appears. He also wants them to score earlier, before matches settle into a pattern of Arsenal possession against a packed defence.

Why he is asking

The results are fine. Arsenal go into the international break six points clear of Manchester City at the top of the Premier League. The Guardian notes that the Hull game was their fourth one-goal league win of the season, and that they won 15 games by a single goal on the way to last season's title.

The goals are the concern. Arsenal have 15 in their first ten league matches. According to the Guardian, that puts them on course to beat the record for the fewest goals scored by a Premier League champion: 67, by Manchester United in 1992-93, the competition's first season. Arsenal have also struggled to score before half-time. Last month they went six matches in all competitions without a first-half goal, and Arteta has asked his players to be more ruthless.

How Decide Football reads it

Arteta has said publicly what the numbers already show. A side that leads the league by six points is still worried that its attack depends too much on control. The next Premier League matches after the break will show whether the message changes anything: more early goals, more shots from second balls and fewer games decided by one goal.
